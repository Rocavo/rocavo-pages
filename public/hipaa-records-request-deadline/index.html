HIPAA records request deadline calculator

Enter the date your practice received a patient's request for their records. The calculator shows the last day to act on it under the HIPAA Privacy Rule, the last day to give the patient an extension statement, and the latest date an extension can run to.

A final rule published on November 19, 2026 shortens the federal time limit from 30 days to 15 calendar days. The rule takes effect on January 19, 2027, and practices must comply by July 19, 2027. The calculator shows the limit that matches the date you enter.

Nothing you enter leaves your browser. This page does not ask for, send or store any patient information.

Extension statement

If you cannot act within the time limit, the rule allows one extension. Within the original time limit you must provide the patient with a written statement of the reasons for the delay and the date by which you will complete your action on the request. The letter below has those two things. Write the reason yourself, and add the patient's name and address after you copy it into your own document.

How the dates are worked out


    
  
  • Requests received on or after July 19, 2027. The practice must act "as soon as practicable, but no later than 15 calendar days after receipt of the request" (45 CFR 164.524(b)(2)(i), as amended).
    • 
  
  • How the days are counted. The rule does not say how to count the days. This calculator counts the day after receipt as day 1, so the last day is the date of receipt plus 15 days. That is the calculator's reading, not a statement in the rule. The rule's preamble says the time period "begins on the date that a covered entity receives the request", and under the strictest reading (the day of receipt as day 1) the last day would be one day earlier. Acting a day before the date shown is safe under either reading.
    • 
  
  • One extension. The practice "may extend the time for such actions by no more than 30 calendar days" (164.524(b)(2)(ii)) and "may have only one such extension" (164.524(b)(2)(ii)(B)). The rule's preamble puts the total at "45 calendar days from receipt". Within the first 15 calendar days the practice must provide the patient with a written statement of the reasons for the delay and the date by which it will complete its action.
    • 
  
  • Requests received before July 19, 2027. The time limit before this rule is 30 days after receipt, with one extension of no more than 30 days and the written statement due within the first 30 days (45 CFR 164.524(b)(2) before amendment). The new text takes effect on January 19, 2027, but practices are not required to comply with it until July 19, 2027, and may comply earlier.
    • 
  
  • Requests received shortly before July 19, 2027. When the 30-day limit would end on or after July 19, 2027, the calculator shows both sets of dates. We searched the rule and found no statement on how a request still open on that date is treated. Acting by the earlier date meets both.
    • 
  
  • Weekends and holidays. The rule counts calendar days. We found nothing in the rule that moves a time limit falling on a weekend or holiday to the next working day, so the calculator does not move it. It tells you when a date falls on a Saturday or Sunday so you can act before it.
    • 
  
  • The clock does not stop. The preamble says time spent getting more information from the patient or checking their identity counts toward the 15 days. Discussing the scope or format of the request "shall not extend the time allowed" (164.524(c)(3)).
    • 
  
  • 15 days is the outer limit. The duty is to act as soon as practicable. Denying a request, in whole or in part, has the same time limit and needs a written denial.
    • 




What this page does not cover


    
  
  • Shorter limits in other laws. Where another federal or state law requires access in less than 15 calendar days, the rule says "that shorter time period is deemed practicable". Some states set shorter limits. This calculator shows the federal HIPAA limit only; check your state's law.
    • 
  
  • Whether a request can be denied, what a denial letter must say, and review of denials.
    • 
  
  • Requests to send an electronic copy to another person or organization. The rule gives these the same 15 calendar days and one 30-day extension, but has its own conditions that this page does not go through.
    • 
  
  • Requests handled by a business associate on your behalf (where the agreement makes the business associate responsible for responding, the preamble says the 15 calendar days begin when the business associate receives the request), fees, and the form and format of copies. For fees, see the fee schedule page builder.
    • 
  
  • Federal holidays are not marked.
