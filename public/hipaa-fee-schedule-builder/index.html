HIPAA fee schedule page builder

If your practice charges for copies of health records, a new federal rule requires you to post a fee schedule. Fill in your own fees below and this page writes the schedule for your website and a version to print for the front desk.

The requirement is new section 45 CFR 164.525, part of a final rule published on November 19, 2026. Practices must comply by July 19, 2027.

This page never suggests a fee. Every amount is one you type. Under HIPAA a fee charged to a patient for a copy of their own records must be reasonable and cost-based and may include only the costs the rule lists (see "What the rule says" below). State law may limit fees further or forbid them.

    4. How the total fee is worked out (optional)

    The rule's preamble expects the schedule to show how the charges above are used to work out the total fee, for example that they are added together. Write this in your own words.

    5. Sections where you entered no fee

    If you leave a fee table empty, the schedule will say that you do not charge for those copies. Tick to confirm that is what you mean.

    What the rule says
    

      
  
    • Who must post. "If a covered entity imposes fees for copies of protected health information requested under this subpart, the covered entity must post a fee schedule on its website, if it has one, and make the fee schedule available to individuals at the point of service and upon request." (45 CFR 164.525). A practice that charges nothing for copies is not given a posting duty by this section.
      • 
  
    • What it must list. "All types of access to protected health information available free of charge", and "Standard fees" for copies provided to the individual or sent where the individual directs (45 CFR 164.524(a) and (d)), for all available electronic and non-electronic forms and formats, and for copies disclosed under the individual's authorization (45 CFR 164.508), where the practice generally charges for them.
      • 
  
    • What "standard fees" means. The rule's preamble describes them as the charges for the components of a fee (labor, supplies, postage, preparing a summary) "and how the covered entity uses those charges to determine the total fee", and says labor may be shown as an hourly charge.
      • 
  
    • What a fee to a patient may include. Only the cost of labor for copying, supplies for a non-electronic copy, postage when the patient asks for a non-electronic copy or summary to be mailed, and labor for preparing a summary or explanation the patient agreed to (45 CFR 164.524(c)(4)(i), as amended). The preamble says the rule does not address what may be charged for copies sent to a person the patient designates.
      • 
  
    • What must be free. No fee when a patient inspects their own information, or gets electronic information held by the practice "using an internet-based method, such as a personal health application" (45 CFR 164.524(c)(4)(ii), as amended).
      • 

    


    What this page does not cover
    

      
  
    • What your fees should be, or whether a fee is reasonable and cost-based.
      • 
  
    • State law. States may cap or forbid these fees, and the stricter limit applies to you. The preamble says other laws "may limit such fees even further".
      • 
  
    • Your Notice of Privacy Practices, which the same rule also changes.
      • 
  
    • Fees for anything other than copies of health records.
      •
